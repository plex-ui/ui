## Examples

### Overview

<ComponentPreview details={<>
  <p>BottomActions holds a screen's main action at its bottom: an onboarding's Continue, a setup step's Next, a form's Create account. The example is a step of a setup flow after the iOS 27 Health app's Choose the Medication Type, measured on the device: the sheet's bar (Back, the medication's name, Close), the symbol's drawing 24pt under the bar's buttons, the title's line 29pt under it at the rows' text, and no description (a step that needs one sets it under the title in the secondary label). Next stays disabled until a form is chosen, and the lists run on under it. Use it for the one action that moves a person on from the screen, with at most a second, quieter one under it (Not now, Skip); actions of the content stay in the content. It is React Native only: the web has no bottom of a screen to stand at.</p>
  <p>On iOS the action is a capsule across the screen, 36pt from its sides and 52pt tall, its bottom just over the home indicator (2pt above the safe area), as iOS 26's own setup screens set it. On Android it is a full-width 48dp button, 16dp from the sides and 16dp above the gesture bar. Each Button inside is a block of the size <code>3xl</code> unless it sets its own <code>block</code> or <code>size</code>; a second action stands under the first, 8 apart. A second Button with no <code>variant</code> of its own takes the platform's: on iOS a glass capsule of the main one's size, as Apple's step-by-step sheets set Skip; on Android a ghost button.</p>
  <p><code>floating</code> lays the actions over the content, a scroll view under them, which then keeps their room at its end so its last line scrolls clear of them. Over the content the actions stand on a soft edge, as a bottom <a href="/react-native/components/toolbar">Toolbar</a> draws: on iOS the screen's color fading in over 27pt above them (with a light blur on the web), on Android the screen's color fading in. <code>background="fade"</code> lets the content run on under the actions, crisp, as TabBar's <code>fade</code>: from the top action's top the screen's color comes in over 60pt to 85% and holds to the screen's edge, with no blur (Android keeps its soft edge). <code>background="none"</code> draws nothing under them, the content running on bare under the actions as on Apple's own screens; a disabled Button still stands on <code>canvasColor</code> on iOS, so the rows do not show through its gray. <code>canvasColor</code> sets the color it fades into, a grouped screen's gray. Without it the actions take their own room at the end of the screen.</p>
  <p><code>hideOnScroll</code> here moves floating actions out of the way as the content scrolls down, as iOS 26 minimizes its bars and Material&apos;s bottom app bar hides on scroll: <code>useHideOnScroll()</code> takes the scroll view&apos;s <code>onScroll</code> and gives <code>hidden</code> for BottomActions. Past 24pt down (<code>threshold</code>) they go, down by their height and fading (300ms on iOS; Material&apos;s 175ms out, 225ms back), and take no presses; on the way up they come back, and at the end of the content too, as Safari&apos;s bar does (<code>atEnd="hide"</code> keeps them away there, for a page whose own actions stand at its end). A bounce past either end moves nothing; with a screen reader on they stay, and with reduced motion they fade where they stand.</p>

## Platforms

| | iOS 26 | Android |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native component | None: the bottom action of iOS 26's setup screens | None: the Plex Android look's bottom button |
