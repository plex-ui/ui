Components take their phone presentation in a window 767px wide or narrower. Inside a PhoneLayout they take it at any width: a Dialog becomes a phone sheet, a Button takes its phone size. For one dialog only, use `layout="phone"` on [Dialog.Content](/components/dialog#api). A Menu opened inside a PhoneLayout is a phone menu that opens in its box, and one opened in a phone dialog is a phone menu too. Popovers, selects and tooltips still open at the end of the page and take the window's presentation; they follow a PhoneLayout in a later release.

## API

### PhoneLayout

Renders a `div` and accepts its props. It marks the box with `data-plex-layout="phone"`: the style sheets apply their phone rules inside such an element at any window width, and their desktop rules (768px and up) do not apply there.

### usePhoneLayout

`usePhoneLayout()` returns whether components where it is called are laid out as on a phone: inside a PhoneLayout or a Dialog with `layout="phone"`, or in a window 767px wide or narrower. On the server it is false outside a PhoneLayout.

## Styling

The box has layout containment, so it is the containing block of the fixed layers of the dialogs inside it, and it clips its overflow as a screen does; a class of your own can change the overflow. Set `--safe-area-inset-top` and `--safe-area-inset-bottom` on the box when the frame draws a status bar or a home indicator: phone sheets and bars read them before the window's safe area.
